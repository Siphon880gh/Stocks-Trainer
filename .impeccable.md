## Design Context

### Users
People learning to read charts and make practice decisions before they sit in front of a real broker. They come from quizzes, case studies, and coaching trees — not from a trading desk job. When they look at a candle, they should recognize it as the same object they will see on TradingView, Thinkorswim, or a retail broker.

### Brand Personality
Calm, exact, desk-like. Three words: **clear, practiced, unhurried**. The interface should feel like a light research blotter with a live tape, not a hacker console and not a generic SaaS dashboard. Confidence comes from familiar market conventions (right-side price, green up / red down, quiet chrome), not from neon or version-number theater.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Light brokerage tape.** Surfaces: cool paper canvas, white panes, hairline `#d1d4dc` borders. Ink `#131722`, muted `#787b86`. Action blue `#2962ff` used sparingly (primary buttons, active nav, links). Semantic green/red only for price direction and correct/incorrect. Typography: Instrument Sans for UI; charts keep the platform stack (Trebuchet / Segoe). Sentence case. No CRT, scanlines, monospace-as-personality, or `SNAKE_CASE` labels in the learner UI.

**References:** TradingView light chart, Thinkorswim / broker quote panels.
**Anti-references:** neon terminal, dark mode with glow borders, uppercase body text, fake system logs, Inter-on-purple SaaS.

### Design Principles
1. **The chart is the product.** Chrome stays quieter than the tape.
2. **Speak like a desk, not a compiler.** Human labels; keep SAMPLE / delayed feed honesty.
3. **Color means price or state.** Blue is action; green/red are market or grade — never decoration.
4. **One place to go next.** Path, quiz, and case flows should show a single clear primary action.
5. **Familiar beats clever.** Right-axis prices, filled candles, light grids, no invented widgets.
